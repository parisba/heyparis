Last month I flew from Tasmania to Rochester, New York, for [NarraScope 2024](https://2024.narrascope.org/). It ran from 21 to 23 June at [The Strong National Museum of Play](https://www.museumofplay.org). NarraScope is a conference about narrative games and interactive fiction, and I had a great time.

## What I did there

I ran a workshop, [Learn the Basics of Writing Branching Narratives with Yarn Spinner](https://2024.narrascope.org/pages/schedule.html), on the Friday. About 40 people came. It covered branching, variables, jumps and commands in [Yarn Spinner](https://yarnspinner.dev), and finished with how to hook a Yarn story into Unity or Godot. I also gave a seminar on using Yarn Spinner for narrative, which about 80 people attended. The people in both rooms ranged from students and solo writers to narrative leads at big studios, and a lot of the questions were about fitting Yarn Spinner into the tools and processes they already use.

## The keynote

Nat Clayton gave the keynote, [Spatial Tales and Highland Songs](https://2024.narrascope.org/pages/schedule.html). Nat worked with inkle on *A Highland Song*, and the talk was about how they used the shape of the landscape and the levels to tell the story, alongside the writing. It was a banger.

![Nat Clayton's keynote title slide on the big screen in The Strong's theatre](/posts/narrascope2024/nat-clayton.jpg#center)

## Talks I liked

nilson carroll's [AI is Obsolete: Generative Glitch Narratives](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bx72uSuJXC4) came out of years of corrupting game ROMs on purpose and treating the results as art. Geoffrey Golden's [First Person Talkers: Simulating Conversation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF2lafKo-Ug) was about writing in-game conversations that feel like talking to a person. Brendan Desilets and Colin Post's [Interactive Fiction in the Library](https://2024.narrascope.org/pages/schedule.html) covered how libraries use interactive fiction for literacy work and community programs.

![Geoffrey Golden at the lectern beside his introduction slide](/posts/narrascope2024/geoffrey-golden.jpg#center)

## The demo room

The demo room was open on Saturday and Sunday, and I spent a lot of time there. You could play a new narrative game or try a tool, then talk to whoever made it. I brought *I Feel Fine*, one of our games, on a handheld, plus a pile of Yarn Spinner stickers. People played it over the two days.

![I Feel Fine running on a handheld gaming PC, with a rubber-banded stack of Yarn Spinner stickers in front](/posts/narrascope2024/i-feel-fine.jpg#center)

All the talks were streamed live for people who couldn't travel. I also went to a few of the Birds-of-a-Feather sessions, which are informal group discussions on a set topic.

The Strong is a museum about the history of play. In the breaks people went off into its collections, which include old text adventures.

## The Interactive Fiction Technology Foundation

NarraScope is run by the [Interactive Fiction Technology Foundation](https://iftechfoundation.org/). The IFTF is a non-profit, and it looks after a lot of what interactive fiction runs on. It maintains [Twine](https://twinery.org/), which is how a lot of people write their first interactive story. It runs the yearly [Interactive Fiction Competition](https://ifcomp.org/). It hosts the [IF Archive](https://ifarchive.org/), which holds decades of games, along with the [IFDB](https://ifdb.org/) and the [IntFiction forum](https://intfiction.org/). It also gives [grants](https://iftechfoundation.org/committees/grants/grants-guidelines/) to people maintaining IF tools. Volunteers do most of this work.

## RIT

While I was in Rochester, my friend [Professor Stephen Jacobs](https://www.rit.edu/magic/affiliate-spotlight-stephen-jacobs) showed me around RIT's [MAGIC Spell Studios](https://www.rit.edu/magic/). Stephen teaches game design and interactive narrative in RIT's School of Interactive Games and Media. He's also a Visiting Scholar at The Strong and has worked on its exhibits.

![The entrance to RIT MAGIC Spell Studios, a long orange-tiled building under a cloudy sky](/posts/narrascope2024/magic-outside.jpg#center)

The building has a sound stage with a big LED wall and a lighting grid across the ceiling. When we walked through, someone had laid out curved camera track on the floor.

![Inside the MAGIC Spell Studios sound stage, with an LED wall, overhead lighting grid and curved camera dolly track on the floor](/posts/narrascope2024/magic-inside.jpg#center)
